Eleven minutes, one batch, charge to drop

Every coffee we sell goes through the same drum on the same morning. Here is one batch of this week's Huila, minute by minute.

  1. 06:00drum 200 °C

    Charge

    Twelve kilos of green coffee fall from the hopper into a drum that has been heating since five. The beans are pale, hard and smell of cut grass.

  2. 06:04bean 155 °C

    Yellowing

    The water cooks out. The beans turn straw-yellow, then tan, and the roastery starts to smell of toast and hay.

  3. 06:08bean 196 °C

    First crack

    Steam breaks through each bean with a crack like popcorn. We turn the flame down and start counting the development time.

  4. 06:11bean 207 °C

    Drop

    Two and a half minutes after first crack, at the colour we chose on the cupping table, the door opens and the batch falls.

06:11 · The drop

Four minutes to stop the roast

The batch pours into the cooling tray. Its arms sweep the beans while a fan pulls cold air down through them, so the roast stops where we chose and not a degree later.

  • 207 → 25 °Cin under four minutes, every batch
  • The chaffthe papery skin the roast lifts off each bean. It is the ash in our name; we collect it and it goes to an allotment down the road.

The week

One roast a week, and nothing waits

  1. Mon

    Orders close

    At 23:59. We roast what was ordered, plus a little for our own counter.

  2. Tue

    Roast day

    Six to eight batches from 06:00, each one logged, tasted and labelled.

  3. Wed

    Rest and cup

    The beans breathe out their CO₂ for a day. We cup every batch before it is packed.

  4. Thu

    Pack and post

    Bagged with the roast date on the front and in the post by 15:00.

  5. Fri

    At your door

    Three days off roast, right at the start of its best fortnight.

Why Tuesday

Coffee tastes best from about the third day after roasting to the third week. We roast on Tuesday so that all of it happens in your kitchen, and none of it on a shelf.

  • 12kg

    The most we put in the drum at once, so every bean sees the same heat.

  • 72h
