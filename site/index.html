Websites that move like what they're about.

Murmuration reads your subject, finds its verb and builds the site in plain HTML, CSS and JS, with real 3D made in code. A roastery roasts. A ledger balances. A tide goes out.

The problem

Ask an AI for an animated landing page and you get the same page every time.

  • a gradient blob
  • drifting particles
  • a globe of dots
  • cards that tilt
  • a glowing orb
  • a marquee of logos
  • steam off a cup
  • equaliser bars

Murmuration starts from the subject instead. It finds what the subject does, the way a roaster watches a curve, a ledger balances, a frame goes up and a tide goes out, and builds the one motion only that subject could have.

How it works

From one sentence to a site that moves.

Six steps, in order. You can stop it at any of them.

  1. 01

    Reads your project

    The product, the brand's colours and fonts, the photos, renders or models that exist, the stack.

  2. 02

    Interviews you

    Two short rounds: what the first five seconds should feel like, then what is real. "You decide" is always an answer, and "just build it" skips the questions.

  3. 03

    Offers directions

    Two or three that genuinely differ, each drawn from your subject. You pick.

  4. 04

    Writes MOTION.md

    The signature, the verb it comes from, an intensity from 1 to 10, the timing tokens and what the page looks like with the motion removed.

  5. 05

    Builds it

    Real content first, then 3D built in code, with a drawn stand-in where WebGL is missing and finished stills under reduced motion.

  6. 06

    Checks it with its own eyes

    Screenshots in both themes, a short laptop window, a phone, no WebGL, reduced motion and a real GPU browser.

Same skill, six worlds

Six one-line briefs.
Six ways of moving.

Each site was built by the skill in a fresh session from the prompt shown, told to decide everything itself. None of them chose the same motion, and none of them reached for particles. The same SKILL.md runs in any agent. Each site was then opened in a real browser and fixed where it broke, which is the skill's last step.

01Coffee roastery

Ember & Ash

It built
A roasting drum, made in code, that turns as you scroll. The beans inside go from green to brown, crack, and pour out into the cooling tray.
Its verb
“The roastery's verb is roast: colour changing with heat over about eleven minutes, once a week.”

Steam curling off a cup, beans floating in space.

The catalogue

Nineteen signatures. Particles are one of them.

The skill picks one per page from your subject and the assets you have, then two to four quiet supporting moves. Each technique has a recipe: what it fits, how to build it without a framework, what it costs and what the page shows at rest.

Scenes and objects

  • A 3D object, turning or relit
  • A 3D world or flythrough
  • A particle cloud that re-forms
  • A globe or map with routes
  • An image sequence scrubbed by scroll
  • Video

Surfaces and fields

  • A shader field: mesh, aurora, liquid metal, dither, halftone, ASCII
  • A generative canvas
  • Physics

Drawing and type

  • Line drawing and mask reveals
  • Type as the image
  • Illustration in motion

Space and structure

  • Depth layers
  • Pinned chapters
  • Image transitions

Interaction and pacing

  • Cursor-led reveals
  • Page choreography
  • A mechanic: hold to advance, a small game
  • Data in motion

Install

Give your next site a verb.

Murmuration is an open agent skill: one folder with a SKILL.md, read the same way by every agent that supports the standard.

Any agent

npx skills add aniketshukla1/murmuration

It asks which agents to install into. Add -a codex (or cursor, gemini-cli, github-copilot) to pick one, and -g to install it for every project.

By hand

Copy skills/murmuration into your agent's skills folder: .agents/skills/ for Codex, Cursor, Copilot, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, Amp and Cline, or .claude/skills/ for Claude Code. Chat apps that take skill uploads, such as claude.ai, take murmuration-skill.zip from the latest release.
